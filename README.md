**Abandoned, But Not Forgotten** is an interactive space exploration and storytelling experience inspired by NASA's challenge to tell the stories of discarded and inactive spacecraft on the Moon, Mars, and beyond.

Every spacecraft has a story. Some completed their missions and remained on distant planetary surfaces. Others continue travelling through space, silently carrying the legacy of human exploration.

This project transforms mission records and scientific information into an immersive digital experience where users can discover the history, purpose, achievements, and final fate of these spacecraft.

## 🎯 The Problem

Space exploration has left behind remarkable evidence of human curiosity and engineering.

Rovers, landers, scientific instruments, and other spacecraft have completed their missions across the Moon, Mars, and deep space. However, their stories are often scattered across technical reports, mission archives, scientific databases, and historical documents.

This makes it difficult for students and the general public to understand:
- What equipment was left behind?
- Why was it abandoned?
- What did it discover?
- Where is it now?
- What does it tell us about humanity's exploration of space?

## 💡 Our Solution

We created an interactive digital archive that combines scientific storytelling, mission data, visual exploration, and educational activities.

Instead of presenting spacecraft history as a collection of technical documents, the platform allows users to explore missions, inspect equipment, compare spacecraft, follow timelines, and discover the stories behind humanity's abandoned space technology.

### 📚 NASA Data

## 🌍 Exploration Across Space

The project explores different destinations and mission categories:

- **The Moon:** Apollo hardware, Surveyor landers, and lunar exploration equipment.
- **Mars:** Pathfinder, Sojourner, Spirit, Opportunity, Phoenix, InSight, and other robotic exploration missions.
- **Deep Space:** Spacecraft that travelled beyond the Earth–Moon system and contributed to our understanding of the universe.

## 🛰️ Scientific Accuracy and Sources

Scientific accuracy is central to this project.

We prioritise official NASA resources and mission documentation when researching spacecraft, mission histories, dates, scientific objectives, and locations.

Key reference resources include:

- [NASA National Space Science Data Center (NSSDCA)](https://nssdc.gsfc.nasa.gov/)
- [NASA Science](https://science.nasa.gov/)
- [NASA Technical Reports Server (NTRS)](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/)
- [NASA Planetary Data System (PDS)](https://pds.nasa.gov/)
- [NASA Solar System Treks](https://trek.nasa.gov/)

Where information is uncertain or precise coordinates are unavailable, the project avoids presenting unsupported assumptions as established facts.

## 🌟 Our Vision

We believe that a spacecraft does not lose its importance when its mission ends.

The equipment left behind on distant worlds represents human achievement, scientific discovery, engineering challenges, and the continuing story of exploration.

Our goal is to make these stories accessible, engaging, and memorable for the next generation of explorers.

**They may have been abandoned, but they should never be forgotten.**
