NASA DATA & SOURCES

WHAT IS REAL, WHERE IT CAME FROM

Beyond the Landing is an educational visualization. Its hotspot layout is illustrative. Optional NASA Photojournal stills (PIA05102, PIA05108) are atmospheric backgrounds only. The experience still works if those images fail to load.

This project separates three kinds of content. Live NASA data is fetched from NASA APIs each time you open a page. Confirmed facts are dates, durations and causes written from NASA/JPL mission records. Storytelling is clearly labelled and never presented as a quote or a finding. Mission Detective and What Remains? use the same static mission records; they do not score answers from NASA APIs.

If an API is slow or offline, the site keeps working: cards fall back to hand-drawn schematic icons and the gallery is simply hidden. A personal NASA API key can be set in js/config.js (the default DEMO_KEY is rate-limited).

Source pages for mission facts

  • Spirit: science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-spirit and JPL “NASA’s Spirit Rover Completes Mission on Mars”.
  • Opportunity: science.nasa.gov/mission/mer-opportunity and NASA’s end-of-mission news release.
  • Surveyor 3: science.nasa.gov/mission/surveyor-3.
  • Sojourner / Mars Pathfinder: nasa.gov “NASA’s First Rover on the Red Planet” and NASA Science Pathfinder pages.
  • Voyager 1 and 2: jpl.nasa.gov/missions/voyager-1 and /voyager-2. Pioneer 10 and 11: NASA Science “10 Things About Pioneer”.
  • Surveyor 1: nasa.gov Surveyor 1 feature and NASA SP-184.
  • Phoenix, InSight, Apollo lunar modules and the remaining Surveyors: NASA/JPL mission summaries, cross-checked against encyclopedic and press sources where a NASA page could not be retrieved directly.

Known limits

  • The small “Where is it now?” maps on mission pages use approximate landing-region coordinates (about ±0.5°). The Hardware Map uses the more precise records in data/objects.geojson; for rovers its pin marks the landing site, not the rover’s final position.
  • Spacecraft distances for the Voyagers and Pioneers are shown as milestones (for example, when each crossed into interstellar space or last made contact), not as live positions.
  • Where sources give slightly different values (for example, the Apollo 12 distance to Surveyor 3, or PST versus UTC landing dates for Spirit and Opportunity), the page states the range or the time-zone caveat.
  • Spirit, Opportunity, and Surveyor 3 were re-checked against NASA Science pages in October 2026. Other missions still use the earlier archive text; see docs/mission-fact-audit.md.
  • NASA Science now reports that Perseverance surpassed Opportunity’s off-Earth driving record on October 11, 2026. This site no longer calls Opportunity’s odometer a current record.

Use of AI

The core concept and structure of this project were independently designed and developed. AI assistance (Claude) was utilized selectively for coding support and drafting, with all facts rigorously checked against official NASA/JPL sources.
